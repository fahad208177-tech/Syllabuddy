# Student testing kit (10 minutes per student)

Goal: 3 to 5 real students try Syllabuddy and say, in their own words, whether it
helped. Their quotes are the strongest evidence for the "Potential Impact"
criterion, and anything that confuses them is a bug to fix before submission.

## Before you start

1. Open the app (the hosted link, or `python run.py` on your laptop).
2. Ask: *"Can I quote what you say, first name only (or anonymously), in a hackathon submission?"* Only quote people who say yes.
3. Ask which exams they're taking (SAT, ACT, AP, A-Levels, anything). Use those.

## The session

Don't explain the app. Hand it over and say:

1. *"Ask it whether something you've been revising is actually on your exam."*
   Let them pick the topic. Note whether the answer was right.
2. *"Ask it about something you think ISN'T on your exam."*
   (If they're stuck: "Is calculus on the SAT?", "Are circles on the PSAT 8/9?", "Is the ratio test on AP Calc AB?")
3. *"Tell it which exams you're taking and when."* Then: *"Ask what you should revise."*
4. *"Ask it to quiz you."* Answer one question.

## Questions to ask afterwards (write their exact words)

1. Did any answer surprise you? Which one?
2. How do you usually find out if something is on the exam?
3. Would you use this while revising? When?
4. What was confusing or annoying?
5. One sentence: what is Syllabuddy, to a friend?
